## Background & Early Explorations -->

I always wanted to invent something new, solve problems and build cool stuff.

At age 10, when I got my first computer I got gifted a book about the visual programming language Scratch. This was my first touch point with software engineering. After building some things in it it annoyed me that I couldn't run these games standalone which spiked the interest in real software development. The first text based programming language I started with was Python, it taught me all the fundamentals and gave me a basic introduction into the new world. Soon I explored the ecosystem more and more but I wanted to make games which got me into Unity and C#, I soon released C# was too complex for me, so I learned it by creating basic desktop applications. In parallel I got invested into web development and created my first frontend but without really understanding what I actually did. 

Step-by-step I began to actually understand how things work and I had my first couple professional paid consulting tasks, helping friends and acquaintances with simple frontend support. I had my first university experience, doing courses at OpenCampus about Game Development, JavaScript, Marketing and Startups along with gaining official certificates where I gained my first ECTs-Points. I also became invested into the startup scene, visiting events like Waterkant and holding my first pitches and speeches.

But my actual career really lifted off until I had my first ever student internship at TypeFox, at age 15. Here I learned the the fundamentals of enterprise level engineering, version control and created the SimpleUI-DSL with a former college.

Soon after I applied, and landed a "Minijob" role at TypeFox, which I worked for 1.5 years, doing mostly frontend architecture and language engineering. After that, school came to an end along the final exams and I mostly did a little freelancing here and there, using the expertise I gained over the last couple years. Then after a short break from all professionalism, spending most of my time with friends and nature. At the start of my first semester, Winter Semester 2025/26 I got back to work and started at TypeFox again, this time in a full professional role, working with clients, large teams and complex projects. All of this while studying full-time for my Bachelors in Computer Science and Sociology.

The way I like to learn has shaped my path, learning by doing. So doing stuff is fundamental for me.
